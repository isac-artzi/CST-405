Lower a Loop by Hand

Labels and jumps on paper, then hand-execute it, then move the test to the bottom and see what you built.

All topics  ›  Topic 4  ›  Activity 1
Format pairsTime 45 minBring paper

The point

Lower a loop by hand before you write the code that does it. The pattern is short enough to memorise and general enough to reuse for the rest of the course.

Do this

  1. Lower this by hand into TAC with labels and jumps:
    i = 0;
s = 0;
while (i < 5) {
    s = s + i;
    i = i + 1;
}
print(s);
  2. Hand-execute it. Make a table with columns for i, s, and the instruction number. Step through until it terminates. What does it print?
  3. Move the test to the bottom — emit the body first, then the condition, then a conditional jump BACK to the top. Hand-execute again with i = 5 initially. What changed? What language construct did you just implement?
  4. Nest two loops on paper. How many distinct labels do you need? What breaks if the label generator returns the same name twice?
  5. Now implement it — the NODE_WHILE case in tac.c — and compare the compiler's output with your handwritten listing.

Deliverable

Your handwritten lowering, the execution trace table, your answer to step 3 naming the construct, and the compiler's .tac next to your version. Feeds Lab Question 17.
