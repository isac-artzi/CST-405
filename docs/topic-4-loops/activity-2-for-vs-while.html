for vs while — a Design Decision

Lower it directly or desugar it? Write both, decide the degenerate cases, then defend your choice.

All topics  ›  Topic 4  ›  Activity 2
Format pairs, at a laptopTime 50 min

The point

for is a design decision, not a feature. Make it deliberately.

Do this

  1. Two implementations, on paper. (a) Lower for directly, with its own case in generateTACStmt. (b) Desugar it in the parser: build a NODE_WHILE whose body is { B; upd; }, preceded by init. Write the TAC each approach produces for the same loop.
  2. Compare the two listings. Are they identical? If not, which is shorter, and does the difference survive optimization?
  3. Decide the awkward cases before you code. What does your language do for each of these, and is it a parse error or a legal program?
    for (; i < 5; i = i + 1) ...     no init
for (i = 0; ; i = i + 1) ...     no condition
for (i = 0; i < 5; ) ...         no update
for (;;) ...                     nothing at all
  4. Implement your choice. Then write a test file that exercises all four cases above and confirm each behaves as you decided.
  5. Argue. In two sentences: which implementation would you ship, and what would change your mind?
There is no wrong answer to step 5

Desugaring keeps the back end smaller and guarantees the two loop forms cannot drift apart. A dedicated case keeps the TAC readable and lets you optimize for specifically later. Real compilers do both. What matters is that you can say which you did and why.

Deliverable

Both TAC listings from step 1, your decision table for the four degenerate forms, a working implementation, and your two-sentence argument. Feeds Lab Question 18.
