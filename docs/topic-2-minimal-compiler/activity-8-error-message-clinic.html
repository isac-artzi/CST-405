Error Message Clinic

Score your diagnostics out of four, rewrite the worst, and swap compilers.

All topics  ›  Topic 2  ›  Activity 8
Format groups of threeTime 45 min

The point

A compiler is a user interface. Its users are programmers, and they meet it almost exclusively through its error messages.

Do this

  1. Collect real ones. Each person writes three broken programs — one lexical error, one syntax error, one semantic error — and runs them through their own compiler. Paste the actual output.
  2. Score every message against four questions: Does it give the LINE? Does it name the OFFENDING THING? Does it say what was EXPECTED? Does it suggest a FIX? Most first drafts score one out of four.
  3. Rewrite the worst three. Aim for four out of four.
    before:  syntax error

after:   ❌ Syntax Error at line 7:
            Missing semicolon after assignment
            💡 Suggestion: add ';' after 'total = 2 + 3'
  4. Implement one of them as a bison error production. See the lecture notes for the shape.
  5. Swap compilers. Run your broken programs through a group member's compiler. Whose messages would you rather receive at 2am?

A harder question, if you have time

What should the compiler do AFTER reporting an error — stop, or keep going? Try both. Feed a file with five errors to each. There is a real trade-off here: keeping going can cascade one mistake into twenty spurious ones, and stopping makes the programmer recompile five times.

Deliverable

A table of your before/after messages with their four-point scores, at least one implemented error production, and a paragraph on the stop-or-continue question. This material is the strongest thing you can put in your Project 2 video.
