Build the Tree, Then Print It

Semantic actions, AST constructors, and a deliberate strdup bug that will save you a week.

All topics  ›  Topic 2  ›  Activity 4
Format pairs, at a laptopTime 50 min

The point

Make the tree real, and make it visible. printAST is the instrument you will use to debug everything else this semester.

Do this

  1. Write the AST constructors in ast.c. Follow the pattern of createNum, which is given. Five functions, three lines each.
  2. Wire them into the grammar actions in parser.y.
  3. Write printAST. Two spaces of indent per level. Recurse into children at level + 1 — except for NODE_STMT_LIST, whose two children print at the SAME level, because a sequence is not a nesting.
  4. Test it against your paper answer from Activity 3. If the printed tree does not match what you drew, one of the two is wrong; find out which before moving on.
  5. Deliberately break it. In createAssign, store the name without strdup: node->data.assign.var = var;. Rebuild and run. Describe what you see and explain why.
Step 5 is the most valuable five minutes of the week

The scanner reuses its buffer for the next token. A name you merely point at will have changed by the time the semantic analyzer reads it. The symptom is an AST that looks randomly corrupted, and it is one of the hardest bugs to find in this course — unless you have seen it once, on purpose, when you knew what you had just done.

Deliverable

A working printAST, its output for the Activity 3 program, and one paragraph describing what step 5 produced and why.
