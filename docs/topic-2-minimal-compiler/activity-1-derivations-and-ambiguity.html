Derivations, Parse Trees, and Ambiguity

Derive by hand, then find two trees for one string — and fix it two different ways.

All topics  ›  Topic 2  ›  Activity 1
Format pairsTime 45 minBring paper

The point

Before touching bison, derive some sentences by hand. Ambiguity is easy to define and hard to recognise, and the only reliable way to recognise it is to have once found two different trees for the same string.

Do this

  1. Derive. Using the starter grammar, write a leftmost derivation for
    int x;
x = 1 + 2;
    Show every step: program ⇒ stmt_list ⇒ ...
  2. Draw the parse tree for that derivation.
  3. Now the interesting one. Take this grammar fragment, which has no precedence declarations at all:
    expr -> expr '+' expr | expr '*' expr | NUM
    Draw two different parse trees for 1 + 2 * 3. Evaluate both. You should get two different numbers.
  4. Fix it two ways. (a) By rewriting the grammar into layers (expr → term → factor). (b) By keeping the ambiguous grammar and adding %left declarations. Write both.
  5. Argue. Which fix would you ship, and why? There is a real answer here and it is not obvious — write down the trade-off you actually believe, not the one you think is expected.
If you are stuck on step 5

Layering is portable: it works with any parser generator, and the grammar itself documents the precedence. Precedence declarations are shorter, easier to change, and keep the grammar readable — but they are a bison feature, and they hide the precedence somewhere other than the rules. Both arguments are good. Pick one and mean it.

Deliverable

A photo or scan of your worked pages, plus a short paragraph answering step 5. This is most of Lab Question 3.
